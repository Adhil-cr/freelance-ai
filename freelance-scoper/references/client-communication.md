# Client message templates

For the onboarding process, kickoff call, communication rules and difficult situations, read `client-onboarding.md` first. This file holds quick message templates.

Principles: short, specific, in writing. Confirm every decision in a message the client can reply "yes" to. Use the client's language and channel (WhatsApp is normal in India), but keep a written trail.

## Discovery questions (pick the ones that matter for this project)

Always:
1. What problem are you trying to solve, and who will use this?
2. What does success look like in 3 months?
3. Is there a deadline, and what is driving it?
4. What is your budget range? (If they will not say, offer two options at different scopes.)
5. Who makes the final decision, and who will give feedback quickly?
6. Do you have branding, content (text, images), or an existing site or app?
7. Do you already have a domain, hosting, or payment gateway account?
8. Who will maintain it after launch?

Add domain-specific questions from the relevant file in `references/domains/`.

## Message templates (adapt tone; keep under 8 lines)

**After the discovery call**
> Hi [Name], thanks for the call today. Here is what I understood: [1 to 3 lines]. First version would include: [list]. Later phases: [list]. I'll send a price and timeline by [day]. Please correct anything I got wrong.

**Sending the proposal**
> Hi [Name], here is the proposal. Summary: [scope in one line], [timeline], [price before GST], 40% advance to start, then milestone payments. It includes 2 rounds of design changes. Anything outside the list is quoted separately before I start it. Happy to walk through it on a quick call.

**Requesting design sign-off**
> Hi [Name], the design prototype is ready: [link]. Please review all screens and send your changes in one list. Once you reply "approved", I'll start the build. Changes after approval are treated as change requests.

**Handling a scope-creep request**
> Good idea. This is outside the agreed scope, so I'll send a short quote for it: about [X] extra hours, [price], [effect on timeline]. If you approve, I'll add it; if not, we continue with the original plan.

**Chasing a late payment (polite, then firm)**
> Hi [Name], a reminder that the [milestone] payment of [amount] was due on [date]. Please send it by [date] so the schedule stays on track. Work resumes once it's received.

**Weekly update**
> Weekly update: done [1 to 2 items]. Next [1 to 2 items]. Need from you: [item] by [date].

## Handling price pushback

1. Anchor at the opening price, not the fair price.
2. Do not discount without removing scope. "I can reach that budget if we start with these features and add the rest in phase 2."
3. Never go below the walk-away floor. If the client cannot meet it, offer a smaller MVP or decline politely.
4. Do not apologise for your price. Explain what the price includes.

## What not to do
- Do not start before the advance.
- Do not accept verbal-only changes.
- Do not send source code or credentials before final payment.
