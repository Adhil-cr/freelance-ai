# Domain: ERP and company internal systems

Use for inventory, purchase, sales, HR, CRM, accounting-adjacent tools, and any software a company's staff use daily.

## Why these projects go wrong
They look like a list of screens but are really a list of **business rules**. Most of the effort is learning how the company works, not coding. Quoting a fixed price before understanding the process is the most common mistake.

## Recommended commercial approach
1. **Paid discovery first** (a few days): interview staff, map the current process (Excel, Tally, paper), list roles, reports and approvals. Deliver a scope document and a phased plan.
2. Then quote **phase 1** (one department or process) as a fixed scope.
3. Add phases only after phase 1 is in daily use.
Never quote the whole ERP from one call.

## Typical modules (list only those the client confirms)
Inventory and stock, purchase orders, sales and invoicing (GST), customers and suppliers, HR and attendance, reports, user roles and approvals, audit log, data import.

## Hidden costs to price
- Data migration and cleaning (messy Excel files take longer than expected)
- Roles, permissions and approval workflows
- Reports and GST-format invoices (accuracy matters, mistakes are costly)
- Training staff and a support period after launch
- Integration with existing tools (Tally, payment, WhatsApp)
- Backup, access control, audit trail

## Questions to ask
1. Which process hurts most today, and what is the cost of that pain?
2. Who uses it daily, who approves, who sees reports?
3. What data exists today and in what form?
4. Which reports or invoices must the system produce?
5. Who decides, who tests, who will support it afterwards?
6. Cloud hosting or inside the company's network?

## Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Scope explosion ("one more module") | Phase plan with a change-request rule |
| Staff do not adopt it | Involve a daily user from the start; short training; simple screens |
| Wrong calculations (stock, tax) | Test with real past data; sign-off per module |
| Dependence on you | Documentation, handover, and an offered support plan |
| Data loss | Backups and a restore test before go-live |
