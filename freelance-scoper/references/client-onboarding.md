# Client onboarding and professional communication

Goal: the client should feel they hired a professional from the first message. Most freelance disputes are communication failures, not coding failures.

## The first 48 hours after the client says yes

1. **Confirm in writing** (within a few hours): scope sheet, price, schedule, what you need from them. Template: `templates/scope-sheet.md`.
2. **Agreement and advance**: send the short agreement (`templates/simple-agreement.md`) and your invoice for the advance. No design work before the advance is received.
3. **Welcome message** with the next three steps and dates.
4. **Kickoff call** booked within the first week.
5. **Create a project folder** (shared drive or notes) holding the scope sheet, decisions log, assets, and links. Share it with the client.

**Welcome message (WhatsApp or email)**
> Hi [Name], welcome aboard and thank you for the trust. Here's what happens next: 1) I've sent the agreement and the advance invoice. 2) Once the advance is in, we do a 30-minute kickoff call on [day]. 3) I'll then share the first design screens by [date]. I'll send a short update every [day]. If anything changes on your side, just message me here.

## Kickoff call (30 minutes)
Agenda, sent the day before:
1. Goal and success in one sentence (5 min)
2. Walk through the scope sheet: what is in, what is out (10 min)
3. Who decides, who gives feedback, how fast (5 min)
4. Assets and access needed (5 min)
5. Schedule, communication rules, next steps (5 min)

After the call, send a summary within 24 hours and ask for a "yes". If it is not in writing, it did not happen.

## Information and access checklist
- Brand assets (logo, colours, fonts), content (text, images), reference apps or sites they like
- Domain, hosting, payment gateway and store accounts: **the client creates them in their own name** and adds you as a user
- Never ask for personal passwords. Use invitations or shared-access features, and remove access at handover
- Test users, sample data, or documents the system must handle
- Names and contact details of the decision-maker and the day-to-day contact

## Communication rules (put these in the welcome message or agreement)
- **One main channel** (for example WhatsApp for quick questions, email for decisions and files).
- **Response time**: you reply within one working day. Say your working hours.
- **Weekly update** on a fixed day: done, next, needs from the client, risks.
- **Decisions in writing**: a short decision log (date, decision, who confirmed). Share it.
- **Feedback in one list**: the client collects all changes into one message per review round.
- **Calls need an agenda; calls end with a written summary.**

**Weekly update format**
> Update for [date]. Done: [1 to 3 items]. Next week: [1 to 3 items]. I need from you: [item] by [date]. Risks: [none / one line]. Overall: [on track / a day or two behind, here's why].

## Handling different clients
| Client type | What they care about | How to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Trust, simplicity, price | Plain language, fewer meetings, show progress early, keep the agreement short |
| Small business owner | Speed, results for customers | Weekly short calls, visual progress, clear who updates content |
| Startup founder | Speed, flexibility, runway | Phased plan, MVP first, clear IP and ownership, be firm about change requests |
| Company / ERP | Process, compliance, sign-off | Written requirements, named approvers, vendor documents (PAN, bank details), purchase order, formal acceptance |

## Professional habits that separate seniors from beginners
- State what you will deliver and when, then deliver it. Under-promise on dates.
- Say "I'll confirm by tomorrow" instead of guessing an answer. Then confirm.
- Never accept scope changes by chat. "Good idea, here's the quote for it" is a complete sentence.
- Do not discount without removing scope. Trade price for scope, not for hope.
- Bad news early, with a plan: "We are two days behind because X. Here is how I'll recover, and here is what I need from you."
- Keep a calm, short tone. Avoid long apologies, and avoid technical jargon unless the client uses it first.
- Record the work: screenshots, short demo videos, dates. They protect you in disputes and build your portfolio (ask permission first).
- Use the same professional email, signature, and invoice layout for every client.

## Difficult situations (short scripts)
**Client goes silent:** "Hi [Name], checking in on the design approval. The project is paused until I hear back, and the delivery date moves by the same number of days. Could you confirm by [date]?"
**Client wants to skip the advance:** "I start every project once the advance is received. It lets me reserve the time. I'm happy to share the invoice now so we can begin this week."
**Client asks for "one small change" repeatedly:** "These are good additions. They are outside the original scope, so I've bundled them into one quote: [hours, price, effect on date]. Shall I add them?"
**Client says the price is too high:** "I understand. Here are two ways to reach your budget: start with [smaller scope] now and add the rest later, or keep the full scope on a longer timeline. Which do you prefer?"
**Payment is late:** "Hi [Name], the [milestone] invoice of [amount] was due on [date]. Could you confirm when I can expect it? I'll continue work once it's received."
**Client is unhappy with the result:** Ask them to point to the agreed acceptance criteria. Fix defects free; price anything new as a change request. Stay factual.

## Handover and closing
- Final demo call, admin guide (one to two pages or a short video), credentials transferred, backups confirmed.
- Acceptance note signed (`templates/` has one in the paperwork guide) and final invoice issued.
- Offer the warranty period and a maintenance plan, ask for a short testimonial, and ask permission to show the work in your portfolio.
