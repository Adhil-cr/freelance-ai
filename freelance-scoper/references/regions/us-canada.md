# Clients in the US and Canada

Last reviewed: 2026-11-10. W-8BEN points checked against Stripe's guide; the rest is general knowledge. **Verify before relying on it. Not legal or tax advice.** Read `international-clients.md` first.

## Market
- Highest rates among common client regions. Benchmarks put US and Canadian freelance developer rates at roughly 2 to 3 times South Asian rates, so pricing at an Indian local rate leaves money on the table. The default multiplier here is a cautious 2.0.
- Clients expect a proposal, a clear timeline, fast replies and professional invoices. Many ask for references or a portfolio.

## Paperwork to verify
- **W-8BEN:** the IRS form a non-US individual gives a US payer to certify foreign status and, if eligible, claim a reduced rate under a tax treaty. Give it before you are paid; without a valid form the payer may withhold at the default rate, often 30 percent. It generally expires after three calendar years, so renew it. Whether withholding applies to work done outside the US depends on how the income is sourced; confirm with a tax professional. Companies use W-8BEN-E instead.
- Treaty claims need a valid taxpayer ID. Check the India-US treaty and your own situation with a CA.
- **Contract:** a services agreement with IP assignment on payment, a confidentiality clause and a payment term (net 15 or net 30 is common).
- **Invoices:** in USD, with your details and bank or Wise details. Add the remittance information the client needs.
- Many clients use platforms and ask you to invoice through them; check their fees and payout times.

## Habits
- Time zones: India overlaps with the US evening and night. Offer fixed overlap windows and written updates.
- Expect clients to ask for fixed-price quotes with a clear scope; they often like a monthly retainer for ongoing work.
- Tipping, benefits and similar employee expectations do not apply to contractors, but be clear that you are an independent contractor.
