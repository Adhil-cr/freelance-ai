# Clients in the UAE and the Gulf

Last reviewed: 2026-11-10. VAT points checked against published accounting guidance (not the FTA site); the Gulf rate multiplier (1.5) is an unsourced estimate. **Verify before relying on it. Not legal or tax advice.** Read `international-clients.md` first.

## Market
- Large Indian diaspora, so clients are often relationship-driven and negotiate hard. Price in AED or USD, and keep the quote valid for a short time.
- Many projects are for small trading, retail, real-estate and service businesses; e-commerce, booking and CRM tools are common. Arabic and English interfaces may both be needed (right-to-left layout is real work; price it).

## Points to verify
- **VAT:** imported services are subject to 5 percent UAE VAT, but the UAE business recipient accounts for it itself (reverse charge). A non-resident freelancer who is not UAE-registered should not charge UAE VAT on the invoice. Put a clause in the contract that the client is responsible for the reverse charge, and keep proof the client is in the UAE (their VAT registration, an email or contract trail). UAE VAT registration for you is generally needed only with a fixed presence there.
- **India side:** the invoice is an export of services; see `international-clients.md`.
- **Payments:** bank transfer, Wise and card payments are common. Payment terms of 30 to 60 days are normal; insist on an advance.
- **Contracts:** English contracts are widely used. Name the governing law and dispute venue. Ask a lawyer to review for larger projects.
- **Hosting and data:** some clients want data kept in the region; ask before choosing a server.

## Habits
- Business days and weekends differ from India's; confirm working days before promising dates.
- WhatsApp is widely used for business, so you can keep a written trail there. Always follow calls with a written summary and ask for a "yes".
- Ramadan changes working hours; plan the schedule around it.
